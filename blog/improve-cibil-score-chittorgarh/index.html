How to Improve Your CIBIL Score Before Applying for a Loan

By Chittorgarh Loans Team📅 15 Oct 2026⏱ 6 min read

Your CIBIL score is one of the first things a bank or NBFC looks at. A few months of careful habits can make a real difference to whether you are approved and what interest rate you are offered.

Quick summary
  • Scores run from 300 to 900. Many lenders prefer 750 or above, but policies differ.
  • On-time payments and low credit card usage matter most.
  • Fix errors in your report before you apply.
  • Avoid applying to many lenders in a short time.

What is a CIBIL score?

A CIBIL score is a three-digit number from 300 to 900 that summarises how you have handled loans and credit cards. Lenders use it to judge how risky it is to lend to you. A higher score generally means easier approval and better terms.

7 steps to improve your score

1. Get your credit report and read it carefully

Look for accounts you do not recognise, wrong payment dates or loans shown as open when they are closed. Errors are more common than people expect.

2. Dispute mistakes quickly

If you find an error, raise a dispute with the credit bureau and inform the lender concerned. Keep your proof, such as closure letters and payment receipts.

3. Pay every EMI and card bill on time

Payment history is the biggest factor. Set up auto-debit or reminders so you never miss a due date, even by a few days.

4. Keep credit card usage low

Using a large share of your card limit can pull your score down. A common guideline is to stay below about 30% of your limit.

5. Clear overdue amounts in full

Where possible, repay dues in full instead of settling for less. An account marked "settled" can count against you with future lenders.

6. Do not close your oldest accounts

A long credit history helps. Keep older cards active with small, regular spends that you repay in full.

7. Apply only when you are ready

Each loan or card application creates a hard enquiry. Many applications in a short time can make you look desperate for credit. Compare options first, then apply to the best fit.

HabitEffect on your score
Paying on time every monthStrongly positive
Using under ~30% of card limitPositive
Many loan applications at onceNegative in the short term
Missed or late paymentsStrongly negative
Settled or written-off accountsNegative and long-lasting

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying to many banks in one week "to see who says yes".
  • Ignoring small unpaid card bills or late fees.
  • Standing guarantor for a loan without tracking its payments.
  • Paying someone to "guarantee" a higher score. Genuine improvement comes from your payment habits.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to improve a CIBIL score?

It depends on your starting point. Fixing a report error can show up within weeks, while building a better payment record usually takes several months of on-time payments.

Does checking my own CIBIL score reduce it?

No. Checking your own score is a soft enquiry and does not reduce it. Lender applications are hard enquiries and can lower it slightly.

Can I get a loan with a low CIBIL score?

Some lenders consider applicants with lower scores, often with higher interest or extra conditions. Approval is always at the lender's discretion.
